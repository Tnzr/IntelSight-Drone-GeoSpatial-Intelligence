# Business and Economic Plan

## 1. Market need

The demand for geospatial intelligence, aerial situational awareness, and proactive monitoring is growing across public safety, private security, critical infrastructure, insurance, and infrastructure inspection. Current solutions are often either static, expensive, or narrow in scope. IntelSight fills a gap by combining drone collection, AI perception, geospatial analytics, and contextual intelligence in a single system.

## 2. Target customer segments

### Public safety and law enforcement

- area monitoring and incident response
- infrastructure and crowd-watch use cases
- anomaly and vehicle correlation under controlled operational models

### Private security and commercial monitoring

- parking lot and asset surveillance
- event monitoring and perimeter intelligence
- campus security and property protection

### Infrastructure and transportation

- road and parking pattern analysis
- zoning and site utilization studies
- aerial route and corridor monitoring

### Insurance and risk assessment

- claims investigation support
- site access and asset validation
- aerial evidence for disputes and risk evaluation

## 3. Product wedge

The company should not begin by selling a broad, vague "AI drone platform." It should begin by selling a narrow, high-value product wedge:

- geospatial evidence capture and pattern-of-life analytics from airport, hotel, parking-lot, and neighborhood aerial missions
- location-context enrichment for vehicle detections
- event and traffic correlation analytics
- geospatial intelligence for operational planning and investigations

## 4. Business model options

### SaaS and data subscriptions

- geospatial intelligence dashboard subscription
- monthly data storage and analytics fees
- access tiers for geofence analytics and event review

### Service model

- drone data collection and analysis as a service
- recurring missions for clients with fixed geofenced zones
- quarterly intelligence reporting and alerting

### Hardware plus software bundle

- initial consumer-drone workflow packages
- open-hardware autonomous stack packages for advanced customers
- integration support for enterprise and agency deployments

## 5. Economic value

The economic case is built on lower cost, better coverage, and better context than static sensors or manual patrols. Drones can cover areas quickly, capture multi-layered evidence, and feed automated analytics. The value is strongest where there are repeated locations, geofenced areas, movement patterns, and repeated events.

### Value sources

- faster mission coverage than manual patrols
- richer evidence than isolated camera snapshots
- reduced operational cost versus frequent helicopter or large field teams
- cross-domain detection linking vehicles, places, and behavior patterns
- continuous improvement from historical datasets that create a proprietary intelligence layer

## 6. Cost structure

### Phase I costs

- drone hardware and accessories
- storage and compute for processing
- labeling, validation, and test datasets
- software engineering and data pipeline engineering
- legal and privacy review

### Phase II costs

- open drone build and flight controller hardware
- companion compute and multi-camera sensors
- ROS2 and PX4 engineering effort
- communication systems and testing infrastructure
- field validation and operational safety engineering

## 7. Market differentiation

IntelSight differentiates itself by combining the following:

- geospatial grounding instead of isolated object recognition
- context-aware analysis of place, time, frequency, and behavior
- phased deployment from existing commercial drones to open autonomous systems
- modular and auditable data pipeline rather than a fully black-box platform

## 8. Risks and mitigations

### Regulatory risk

- mitigate with legal review, data minimization, and operational policy controls

### Privacy risk

- mitigate with no facial recognition and strict retention controls

### Technical risk

- mitigate with phased rollout and measurable field benchmark targets

### Commercial risk

- mitigate with narrow initial market wedge and clear ROI story for service delivery

## 9. Recommendation

The right first business move is to sell a geospatial intelligence service for recurring operational monitoring and contextual vehicle-to-location analysis. This provides revenue while the team builds the deeper autonomy stack. The strategic value is in turning the dataset into a proprietary map of patterns, places, and repeated observations over time.

## 10. Long-term strategic upside

The real competitive advantage is not just license plate detection. It is the creation of a historical geospatial intelligence layer that associates movement, places, and routines over time. The long-term value is a platform that can support autonomous search, response, anomaly warning, and mission intelligence across many environments.
